This part started with requirements rather than a suggested dollar figure. The needs assessment identified the billing entity, spending authority, continuity expectations and reporting readiness. Where expectations conflicted, the task was to obtain a decision, not to promise that one threshold could satisfy both.

The two-team scenario showed why a metered budget does not reserve a team's share of included credits. Included-usage controls and metered budgets solve different problems. Centralized or delegated ownership changes who makes decisions, but each control still needs an accountable owner.

The output is a decision brief: what is covered, who approves spending, which work may stop, who responds and when exceptions are reviewed. It gives the administrator acceptance criteria for implementation.

## Key Takeaways

- Discover the operating model before prescribing controls or amounts.
- Record license spending and metered spending separately.
- Choose included-usage controls for pool fairness and metered budgets for additional charges.
- Document continuity choices, assignments, exceptions and escalation.
- Use a provisional baseline only within approved financial exposure; forecasts do not authorize spending.

Part 3 turns this brief into a pilot, an operating loop and accountable reporting.
